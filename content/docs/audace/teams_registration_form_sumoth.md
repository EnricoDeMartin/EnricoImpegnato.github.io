## Project Definition
This year the team's objective is to refit our last edition's Moth Neverina, registering it in the UPG category.
This decision comes from the following observations regarding last year's work:
- The boat's construction revealed to be structurally sound, with no major damage sustained during the 2026 SuMoth Challenge, thus remaining seaworthy.
- We think that its hull design will be still relevant at the SuMoth 2027 edition, with its good compromise between strength and optimization.
To push even further the capabilities of this platform, this year team's goals are going to be the following:
- Reinforce the transom. This because the connection between the transom and the rest of the hull form a 90° angle, that increases the stress at the junction. Also, during construction, we found deficiency during the hull assembly; in fact we found during maintenance, that the 90° supports used to aid the connection between hull and transom did not properly get in contact with each other.
  In the end, we also aim to strengthen the layup of the transom's panel, because we found that the L-shaped aluminum gantry's fittings pressed into the core thus collapsing it and leaving a trough.
- Produce new single-piece wings, discarding the old assembly-based design with the aim of achieving the following benefits: 
	- Decrease in weight
	- Decrease assembly related issues and imperfections
	- Have smoother and more aerodynamic surfaces and fittings
	- Use less structural binders and glues
  All without compromising on structural strength
- Optimize the spreaders's layup, to reduce weight without compromising on rigidity  
- Produce a new Gantry, refining the shape with parametric optimization for decreasing the drag during the dislocating phase, reduce the weight and keeping the structural rigidity that the old one had.
- Develop a new horizontal foil pair, to increase the efficiency reducing the expanded area, so that they work in a higher speed range. This in the end to widen the possible configurations to adopt in different weather conditions.
- Evaluate the current vertical foils's efficiency verifying that the position of the two horizontals does not create issues regarding negative interaction caused by turbulent inflow from the main foil's trail to the rudder.
- Importing the physic model of the Moth into Gomboc's VPP (from SumToZero), to create a digital twin of our boat and validate all the above design optimizations.
- Reshaping of the running rigging that travels along and aside the wings, to optimize spacing, facilitate maintenance and aid the skipper during the race.
- Consolidate the electronic "box" (position, speed and attitude tracker) to improve its functionalities and reduce its footprint, to aid the integration onboard.
- Add electronic H.I.Ds to aid the skipper during the regatta e.g. by displaying valuable data or to configure the electronics's configurations while sailing.
- Terminate development and implement a new wind intensity and direction sensor (anemometer)
- Realize a fully-electronic flight control system, that is tightly integrated with the already on board electronics so that its behavior can be optimized taking into account the data coming from other sensors
- With the successful realization of the electronics mentioned above, the cross-validation of the data captured by the electronic's onboard system and the results provided by the aforementioned VPP.
